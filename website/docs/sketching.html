User manual / Sketching

Sketching

Starting and ending a sketch

Click a plane or a flat face, then click Sketch (Sketch tab), or right-click a face ▸ Sketch. Clicking a sketch tool such as Circle with no sketch open also starts a 2D sketch: pick a plane or flat face and the tool is ready. (For sketches in space, see the 3D Sketches chapter.) You can also start a feature such as Extrude without a sketch: RazorCAD asks you to pick a plane or face, you sketch, and when you exit the sketch the feature continues. If the profile is open you can keep editing or abort and keep the sketch.

  • Esc ends the active sketch tool first; pressing it again closes the sketch and keeps it.
  • Enter does not leave the sketch.
  • The ✔ in the confirm corner, or Exit Sketch, closes the sketch.
  • Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo inside the sketch.

The sketch tool wheel

While sketching, press and hold the right mouse button. After a moment, or as soon as you start to drag, a wheel of sketch tools appears around the pointer. Keep the button held, move toward a tool (it highlights) and release to activate it. Releasing in the centre or pressing Esc cancels. A quick right click (press and release without moving) still opens the right-click menu.

Defined and under-defined sketches

Sketches are made of points, lines, arcs and circles held in place by relations and dimensions. A numerical constraint solver keeps them satisfied.

  • Geometry is blue when under-defined and black when fully defined.
  • The Property panel shows the remaining degrees of freedom.
  • An under-defined sketch shows (-) in the tree. Construction lines only need to be positioned.

Sketch orientation

In the sketch Property panel, Rotate 90° turns the sketch axes about the sketch normal, and Flip Side looks at the sketch from the other side of the plane. Both are saved with the sketch.

Dimension

Click a line, circle or arc (or two entities), then click to place the dimension.

  • Moving the mouse switches between horizontal, vertical and aligned.
  • Two lines give an angle; a point and a line give a perpendicular distance.
  • Values accept equations, such as width/2.
  • A dimension that would over-define the sketch is added as a driven (reference) dimension.
  • Circles and arcs: dimension from a circle or arc to another circle, arc, point or line by clicking both. The Modify box offers Center (centre to centre), Min (nearest) and Max (farthest). Min also measures the gap between concentric circles. Switching keeps the geometry in place and shows the new value.
  • Outside references: you can also click model edges, model vertices or entities of earlier sketches. RazorCAD creates linked construction geometry and dimensions to it, so the dimension follows the model. Example: "keep this hole 25 mm from the right edge" still holds after the width changes.

Trim

Power trim: hover over a segment and it turns red; click to remove it up to the nearest intersections. Lines are shortened or split, circles become arcs, and new end points get on-curve relations.

Offset

Click a line, arc or circle and its whole connected chain is offset. Move the mouse to the side and distance you want and click, then type the exact distance. Corners are re-trimmed or extended.

  • You can also click a model edge (with Select chain, the connected edges lying in a plane parallel to the sketch, such as a face outline) or a curve of another sketch.
  • Linked offset (default): the offset follows its source, including model edges when the model changes, and one dimension drives the chain (relation "Offset ⇉").
  • Untick it for a single offset: plain geometry with no link.
  • While the tool is active, the faces the sketch plane cuts are highlighted and the cut outline is drawn in teal. Clicking it offsets the whole outline.

Project Edges

Click a model edge, or an entity of an earlier sketch, to project it into this sketch. Or select edges first and then click Project Edges (or press E): they are projected at once. Projected geometry is drawn in purple, is linked and updates when the source changes (relation "On Edge ⇲"). The ends of projected lines are joined to each other and to sketch geometry already there, so the edges of a face give a closed outline ready to extrude. While the tool is active, the outline where the sketch plane cuts the bodies is drawn in teal; clicking it converts the whole outline. This works for solids and surfaces.

Spline

Click the points the curve runs through (they snap like line points), then double-click, Enter or Esc to finish, or click the first point again to close it. Drag a point to reshape the curve, or drag the curve to move it; deleting a point takes it out of the spline. Splines make profiles on their own or with lines and arcs and are exact curves for extrudes, revolves, cuts, lofts and sweep paths. Lines can be trimmed where they cross a spline.

Import DXF

Import DXF brings a DXF file into the open sketch: lines, arcs, circles, polylines with arcs, ellipses and elliptical arcs, splines, points and blocks. The file's units are converted to millimetres. You can scale it and place it as in the file, centred, or by its corner.

Text

Click Text on the Sketch tab, then click to place. The panel sets the text, font (the PC's installed fonts, and the DejaVu Sans, Sans Mono and Serif fonts that come with RazorCAD), height (capital-letter height), angle, bold and italic. A font a PC doesn't have is drawn in the DejaVu font most like it. Drag the text to move it. Text extrudes as a boss or cut; text inside an outline is cut out of it. To put text on a curved face, use Wrap (Features chapter).

Relations

Select entities (Ctrl+click for several). The Property panel lists the relations that apply:

SelectionRelations offered
1 lineHorizontal, Vertical, Fixed
2 or more pointsCoincident, Horizontal, Vertical

Point + lineCoincident (on line), Midpoint
Point + circle/arcCoincident (on curve)

Circles/arcsEqual, Concentric, Tangent
2 points + lineSymmetric (about the line)

Relations are shown as small green glyphs next to the geometry. Click a glyph to select it, then press Del to remove it. Hovering a glyph highlights the entities it ties together. Redundant or conflicting relations are refused with a message.

How profiles are found

Profiles are found automatically from all non-construction geometry and sent to the kernel as exact lines, arcs and circles. Intersections are split, dangling lines are ignored and nested loops become holes. A full circle (or arcs that make one) becomes a single closed curve, so an extruded circle has one continuous cylindrical face.

Showing and hiding sketches

Sketches absorbed by a feature are hidden by default. Right-click a sketch in the tree ▸ Show / Hide, or use its eye in the tree.

Change Sketch Plane

Right-click a sketch ▸ Change Sketch Plane… and pick another plane or flat face for it, for example to repair a sketch whose plane was deleted ("The reference plane is missing"). Its curves keep their sketch coordinates. Only planes above the sketch in the tree are offered.
